The '''2019 FIA Formula 2 Championship''' was the fifty-third season of the second-tier of [[Formula One]] feeder championship and also third season under the moniker of [[FIA Formula 2 Championship]], a [[motor racing]] championship for [[Formula Two|Formula 2]] cars that is sanctioned by the [[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]] (FIA). It is an [[open-wheel racing]] category that serves as the second tier of [[formula racing]] in the [[FIA Global Pathway]]. The category was run in support of the [[F1 2019|2019 FIA Formula 1 World Championship]].

[[George Russell (racing driver)|George Russell]] was the reigning drivers' champion, having won the title at the final round of the 2018 championship in [[Yas Marina Circuit|Abu Dhabi]]. Russell would drive in [[Formula One]] for [[Williams Grand Prix Engineering|Williams]] in 2019.<ref name="russell"/>{{efn|name=defending champion|Under the series' sporting regulations, the defending drivers' champion is not permitted to continue racing in the championship, so Russell would not be able to defend his title.}} [[Carlin Motorsport|Carlin]] were the reigning teams' champions, having secured their first Formula 2 title in Abu Dhabi. [[ART Grand Prix]] driver [[Nyck de Vries]] won the drivers' championship after the win in the Feature race at Sochi. In the teams' championship [[DAMS]] secured their first team title over [[Virtuosi Racing|UNI-Virtuosi Racing]] after the win in the Feature race at Abu Dhabi.

The season was marred by the death of French driver [[Anthoine Hubert]] during the feature race of the [[2019 Spa-Francorchamps FIA Formula 2 round|Spa-Francorchamps round]] on 31 August 2019. Hubert's death was the first fatality for a driver competing in FIA-sanctioned feeder series racing since [[Henry Surtees]]'s fatal crash at [[Brands Hatch]] in [[2009 Brands Hatch Formula Two round|2009]] in the [[FIA Formula Two Championship (2009-2012)|FIA Formula Two Championship]].

The [[Formula One]] theme song composed by [[Brian Tyler]], which debuted in the [[2018 Formula One World Championship|2018 F1 season]], would now be used in Formula 2 broadcasts.

The season would see 8 different winners, with the top three in the championship, De Vries, [[Nicholas Latifi]], and [[Luca Ghiotto]], each winning 4 races. 3 races were won by [[Jack Aitken]], and 2 races were won by Hubert, [[Sérgio Sette Câmara]], and [[Nobuharu Matsushita]]. The only other race winner was rookie [[Mick Schumacher]].

===Team changes===
[[File:FIA F2 Austria 2018 Nr. 01 Markelov.jpg|thumb|[[Russian Time]] withdrew from the championship at the end of the 2018 season.]]
[[Russian Time]] left the championship after six years competing in Formula 2 and its predecessor, the [[GP2 Series]]. Their entry and assets were sold to [[Virtuosi Racing]], who had operated the team. The new entry competes under the name "UNI-Virtuosi Racing".<ref>{{cite news |last1=Benyon |first1=Jack |title=UNI-Virtuosi replaces Russian Time in Formula 2 |url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/uni-virtuosi-replaces-russian-time/4308560/ |access-date=4 December 2018 |work=[[motorsport.com]] |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=4 December 2018 |archive-date=8 November 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201108130909/https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/uni-virtuosi-replaces-russian-time/4308560/ |url-status=dead }}</ref><ref>{{cite news |last1=Benyon |first1=Jack |title=Russian Time sells Formula 2 team |url=https://www.motorsport.com/au/fia-f2/news/russian-time-formula-2-team-sale/4303584/ |access-date=25 November 2018 |work=[[motorsport.com]] |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=25 November 2018 |archive-date=25 November 2018 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20181125162408/https://www.motorsport.com/au/fia-f2/news/russian-time-formula-2-team-sale/4303584/ |url-status=dead }}</ref> [[Fortec Motorsport]]s had been granted an entry to the Formula 2 grid in 2018, but were later given permission to defer their entry until 2019.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/134519/fortec-f2-entry-on-hold-for-one-year|title=Fortec Formula 2 entry on hold for one year|first=Alex|last=Kalinauckas|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=24 February 2018|access-date=24 February 2018}}</ref> However, they were subsequently omitted from the draft entry list for the 2019 championship.<ref name="numbers"/>

[[Arden International]] entered into a partnership with Mercedes-affiliated team [[HWA Team|HWA Racelab]].<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.fiaformula2.com/News-Room/News/2019/01_january/Arden-and-HWA-to-initiate-F2-co-operation/|title=Arden International Motorsport and HWA Racelab to initiate Formula 2 co-operation|work=fiaformula2.com|publisher=Formula Motorsport Limited|date=21 January 2019|access-date=21 January 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190402021714/http://www.fiaformula2.com/News-Room/News/2019/01_january/Arden-and-HWA-to-initiate-F2-co-operation/|archive-date=2 April 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Charouz Racing System]] formed a partnership with [[Sauber|Sauber Motorsport]], which ran [[Alfa Romeo in Formula One|Alfa Romeo's team]] in Formula 1.<ref name="numbers"/> The Sauber-Charouz partnership is not affiliated with Alfa Romeo's Formula 1 entry.

Artem Markelov made a one-off return in Monaco as a replacement for Jordan King at MP Motorsport, who was competing in the [[2019 Indianapolis 500]] with [[Rahal Letterman Lanigan Racing]].<ref>{{cite news |last1=Allen |first1=Peter |title=Artem Markelov returns to F2 for Monaco as substitute for Jordan King |url=http://www.formulascout.com/artem-markelov-returns-to-f2-for-monaco-as-substitute-for-jordan-king/50564 |access-date=18 May 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout}}</ref> After collecting twelve penalty points on his racing license for incurring three Virtual Safety Car infringements during the Paul Ricard feature race, Mahaveer Raghunathan was banned from the series for the Red Bull Ring round.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Lumb |first1=Tim |title=Raghunathan race ban tops Paul Ricard F2 and F3 penalty list |url=http://www.formulascout.com/raghunathan-given-f2-race-ban/51584 |access-date=23 June 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=23 June 2019}}</ref> Raghunathan's MP seat was filled in Austria by [[2018 Indy Lights]] champion and [[2019 IndyCar Series]] driver [[Patricio O'Ward]].<ref name="Ward"/>

Shortly after the Paul Ricard round, Arjun Maini was announced to be replacing Dorian Boccolacci at Campos Racing for the Red Bull Ring and Silverstone rounds.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.fiaformula2.com/News-Room/News/2019/06_June/Arjun-Maini-returns-to-F2-with-Campos-Racing/|title=Arjun Msini returns to F2 with Campos Racing|publisher=[[FIA Formula 2 Championship]]|access-date=25 June 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190626071735/http://www.fiaformula2.com/News-Room/News/2019/06_June/Arjun-Maini-returns-to-F2-with-Campos-Racing/|archive-date=26 June 2019|url-status=dead}}</ref> [[Marino Sato]] replaced Maini prior to the Spa round.<ref name="Sato">{{cite news |last1=Benyon |first1=Jack |title=Campos signs fourth different driver of 2019 |url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/campos-marino-sato-fourth-driver/4521464/ |access-date=27 August 2019 |work=[[motorsport.com]] |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=27 August 2019 |archive-date=27 August 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190827121208/https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/campos-marino-sato-fourth-driver/4521464/ |url-status=dead }}</ref>

After encountering funding problems, Ralph Boschung left Trident prior to the Red Bull Ring round. Former [[GP3 Series|GP3]] driver [[Ryan Tveter]] joined the team in Boschung's place.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/tveter-boschung-trident-red-bull-ring-debut/4482984/|title=Tveter to make F2 debut with Trident|last=Khorounzhiy|first=Valentin|work=[[Motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=27 June 2019|access-date=27 June 2019}}</ref> Tveter was later replaced by Boccolacci. Boschung returned to the team ahead of the Hungarian round.<ref name="Boschung return"/>

BWT Arden was represented only by Tatiana Calderón at Monza after Anthoine Hubert's fatal accident.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Benyon |first1=Jack |title=Hubert's Arden team to run single F2 car at Monza |url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/arden-single-car-monza-hubert-calderon/4526870/ |access-date=2 September 2019 |work=[[motorsport.com]] |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=1 September 2019 }}{{Dead link|date=September 2026 |bot=InternetArchiveBot }}</ref> Juan Manuel Correa, who was also involved in the incident, was forced to miss the rest of the season due to injuries.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Malsher |first1=David |title=Correa begins recovery after horror shunt at Spa |url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/correa-recovery-spa-horror-shunt/4527563/?nrt=73 |access-date=2 September 2019 |work=[[motorsport.com]] |publisher=[[Motorsport Network]] |date=2 September 2019 |archive-date=14 December 2019 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191214184622/https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/correa-recovery-spa-horror-shunt/4527563/?nrt=73 |url-status=dead }}</ref> Sauber Junior Team by Charouz did not replace him at Monza, running only Callum Ilott. Giuliano Alesi's car was impounded by Belgian authorities as part of their investigation into the accident at Spa, limiting Trident to a single entry for the next round. Ralph Boschung was stood down to allow Alesi to compete.<ref name="Crash Media Group"/>

For the Sochi and Yas Marina rounds, [[Matevos Isaakyan]], who raced in the [[2018 European Le Mans Series]], took Juan Manuel Correa's seat at Sauber Junior Team by Charouz.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Costa |first1=Massimo |title=Isaakyan a Sochi e Yas Marina, debutto in F2 con Sauber by Charouz |url=https://www.italiaracing.net/Isaakyan-a-Sochi-e-Yas-Marina-debutto-in-F2-con-Sauber-by-Charouz/239499/4 |access-date=18 September 2019 |work=ItaliaRacing.net |publisher=Inpagina |date=17 September 2019 |language=it|trans-title=Isaakyan debuts in F2 for Sauber by Charouz in Sochi and Yas Marina}}</ref> Artem Markelov replaced Anthoine Hubert at BWT Arden for the same rounds, although the Russian raced with #22 as the #19 had been retired for the remainder of the season in honour of the late French driver.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Allen |first1=Peter |title=Arden signs Markelov to take Hubert’s place in F2 line-up |url=http://www.formulascout.com/arden-signs-markelov-to-take-huberts-place-in-f2-line-up/54798 |access-date=24 September 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=24 September 2019}}</ref>

==Calendar==
The following twelve rounds were scheduled to take place as part of the 2019 championship. Round 9 was abandoned. Each round consisted of two races: a Feature race, which was run over {{convert|170|km|mi|1|abbr=on}} and included a mandatory pit stop; and a Sprint race, which was run over {{convert|120|km|mi|0|abbr=on}} and did not require drivers to make a pit stop.{{efn|The Feature and Sprint races are time-certain. In the event that the full race distance cannot be completed, the Feature race will end after one hour and the Sprint race after forty-five minutes.}} The 2019 calendar retained the same twelve rounds from the 2018 season.

==Championship standings==
===Scoring system===
Points were awarded to the top 10 classified finishers in the Feature race, and to the top 8 classified finishers in the Sprint race. The pole-sitter in the feature race also received four points, and two points were given to the driver who set the fastest lap inside the top ten in both the feature and sprint races. No extra points were awarded to the pole-sitter in the sprint race as the grid for the sprint race were based on the results of the feature race with the top eight drivers having their positions reversed.<ref name=Regulations>{{cite web|title=2019 FIA Formula 2 Sporting Regulations|url=https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2019_fia_f2_sporting_regulations_v2_11-05-19.pdf|work=[[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]]|pages=3-4|date=11 May 2019|access-date=17 March 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221011223201/https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2019_fia_f2_sporting_regulations_v2_11-05-19.pdf|archive-date=11 October 2022|url-status=dead}}</ref>
