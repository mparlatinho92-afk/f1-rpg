The '''2019 Euroformula Open Championship''' was a multi-event [[Motorsport#Motor racing|motor racing]] championship for single-seat [[Open-wheel car|open wheel]] [[Formula racing|formula racing cars]] that held across [[Europe]]. The championship features drivers competing in [[Formula Three|two-litre Formula Three]] racing cars built by Italian constructor [[Dallara]] which conform to the technical regulations, or formula, for the championship. It was the sixth [[Euroformula Open Championship]] season.

For the first time since its inception, the championship featured multiple power unit manufacturers, allowing the use of [[Mercedes-Benz in motorsport|Mercedes]] and [[Volkswagen]] engines. It was intended to have equalised performance of the powerplants, in terms of both power and torque like in the GT3 racing.<ref name="Calendar">{{cite news |last1=Allen |first1=Peter |title=Euroformula Open to permit VW and Mercedes engines in 2019 |url=http://www.formulascout.com/euroformula-open-to-permit-vw-and-mercedes-engines-in-2019/44735 |access-date=2 September 2018 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=31 August 2018}}</ref> But the equalisation was not successful as the teams that used Toyota engine which was tuned by Piedrafita had a lack of power in comparison with Mercedes and Volkswagen engines. The situation led to the withdrawal of the teams after the first round and switching to the Mercedes and Volkswagen engine prior round at Spa.<ref name="Colapinto">{{cite news |last1=Wood |first1=Ellot Wood |title=Fernando Alonso protege Franco Colapinto joins Euroformula Open, RP Motorsport returns |url=http://www.formulascout.com/franco-colapinto-ef-open-rp-motorsport/51169 |access-date=8 June 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=8 June 2019}}</ref>

[[Motopark Academy|Team Motopark]] driver [[Marino Sato]] won the title after the first race at Barcelona, having won eight races, including series of six wins in row in Spa, Hungaroring and Spielberg, while his team clinched the title after the second Spielberg race.<ref>{{cite news |last1=Wood |first1=Elliot |title=How to win a F3 title: Marino Sato breaks down his breakout year |url=http://www.formulascout.com/how-to-win-a-f3-title-marino-sato-breaks-down-his-breakout-year/54760 |access-date=24 September 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=23 September 2019}}</ref> Top rookie [[Liam Lawson]] was Sato's closest challenger, winning four races throughout the season. His fellow [[Red Bull Junior Team]] stablemate [[Yuki Tsunoda]] won a race at the [[Hockenheimring]], [[Teppei Natori]] was victorious in the second race at [[Circuit de Barcelona-Catalunya|Catalunya]], [[Billy Monger]] became the first double-amputee to win a race in single-seaters when he won the [[2019 Pau Grand Prix|Pau Grand Prix]], and [[Toshiki Oyu]] won both races at a one-off appearance in [[Silverstone Circuit|Silverstone]]. A standalone winter series round was also held in February at [[Circuit Paul Ricard]], with [[Linus Lundqvist]] and Formula 2 driver [[Nobuharu Matsushita]] emerging victorious.<ref name="WinterSeries">{{cite web|url=https://formulascout.com/matsushita-lundqvist-ef-open-winter-series/48534|title=Matsushita and Lundqvist win EF Open Winter Series races at Paul Ricard|website=Formula Scout|last=Wood|first=Ida|date=23 February 2019|access-date=19 January 2025}}</ref>

==Race calendar and results==
An eight-round provisional calendar was revealed on 31 August 2018.<ref name="Calendar"/> The calendar features six circuits from 2018 schedule. While [[Autódromo do Estoril]] and [[Circuito de Jerez]] are not present in the current version of the calendar, [[Hockenheimring|Hockenheim]] made its debut as a Euroformula Open Championship round. The date of the Spa round was altered on 29 November 2018.<ref>{{cite news |title=New date (7-9 June) for the Spa-Francorchamps round |url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3403/new-date-7-9-june-for-the-spa-francorchamps-round |work=euroformulaopen.net |publisher=GT Sport}}</ref> On 10 December 2018 was announced that [[Pau Grand Prix]] will make debut in the extended nine-round Euroformula Open Championship schedule.<ref>{{cite news |title=Pau added to Euroformula Open's exciting 2019 calendar! |url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3409/pau-added-to-euroformula-open-s-exciting-2019-calendar |access-date=10 December 2018 |work=euroformulaopen.net |publisher=GT Sport |date=10 December 2018}}</ref>
