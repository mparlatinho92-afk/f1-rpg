The '''2018 FIA Formula 2 Championship''' was the fifty-second season of the second-tier of [[Formula One]] feeder championship and also second season under the moniker of [[FIA Formula 2 Championship]], a [[motor racing]] championship for [[Formula Two|Formula 2]] cars that is sanctioned by the [[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]] (FIA). It is an [[open-wheel racing]] category that serves as the second tier of [[formula racing]] in the [[FIA Global Pathway]]. The category run in support of the [[2018 FIA Formula One World Championship]], with each of the twelve rounds running in conjunction with a [[Grand Prix motor racing|Grand Prix]].<ref name="rebranded">{{cite news|url=https://speedcafe.com/gp2-series-renamed-fia-formula-2/|title=GP2 Series renamed FIA Formula 2|first=Tom|last=Howard|work=[[Speedcafe|speedcafe.com]]|date=10 March 2017|access-date=10 March 2017|archive-date=20 November 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20191120093104/https://www.speedcafe.com/2017/03/10/gp2-series-renamed-fia-formula-2/|url-status=live}}</ref> It was the first FIA Formula 2 season to feature a new chassis and engine package.

[[Charles Leclerc]] was the reigning drivers' champion, having won the title at the penultimate round of the 2017 championship in [[Circuito de Jerez|Jerez de la Frontera]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/132272/leclerc-seals-f2-title-with-jerez-win|title=Leclerc seals F2 title with Jerez win|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=7 October 2017|access-date=8 October 2017|first=Alex|last=Kalinauckas}}</ref> Leclerc subsequently secured a Formula 1 drive with the Alfa Romeo Sauber team in 2018<ref name="it is i, leclerc!">{{cite news|last1=Noble|first1=Jonathan|title=Sauber confirms Ericsson alongside Leclerc for 2018|url=https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/sauber-confirms-ericsson-leclerc-for-2018-f1-season-985150/|access-date=2 December 2017|work=[[motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=2 December 2017|archive-date=17 June 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180617165250/https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/sauber-confirms-ericsson-leclerc-for-2018-f1-season-985150/|url-status=dead}}</ref>{{efn|Under the series' sporting regulations, the defending drivers' champion is not permitted to continue racing in the championship, so Leclerc would not be able to defend his title.}} [[Russian Time]] were the reigning teams' champions, having secured their first Formula 2 title at the final round of the 2017 championship in [[Abu Dhabi]].<ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/133286/leclerc-wins-f2-finale-after-lastlap-contact|title=Ferrari junior Leclerc wins F2 finale with a stellar last lap move|first=Alex|last=Kalinauckas|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=26 November 2017|access-date=27 November 2017}}</ref> [[George Russell (racing driver)|George Russell]] won the drivers' championship, finishing 68 points ahead of [[Lando Norris]]. In the teams' championship, [[Carlin Motorsport|Carlin]] prevailed over [[ART Grand Prix]] by 31 points. Champion Russell won 7 races during his season, [[Alexander Albon]] took 4 race victories, whilst 3 races were won by [[Nyck de Vries]], [[Artem Markelov]], 2 wins went to [[Antonio Fuoco]], driver of new [[Charouz Racing System]] team. Other winners, who were victorious once - its runner-up Norris, [[Jack Aitken]], [[Maximilian Günther]], [[Nicholas Latifi]], and [[Tadasuke Makino]].

==Changes for 2018==
===Technical changes===
[[File:FIA F2 Austria 2018 Nr. 07 Aitken (3).jpg|thumb|230px|right|The new [[Dallara F2 2018]] made its début in 2018.]]
The season saw the introduction of a brand new chassis and engine package, the biggest change in the sport's history.<ref>{{cite web|title=GP2 aiming for V6 switch, but not wider tyres for 2018 car|url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/gp2-aiming-for-v6-switch-but-not-wider-tyres-for-2018-car-859721/|last=Khorounzhiy|first=Valentin|work=[[motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=16 December 2016|access-date=16 December 2016}}</ref> The [[Dallara GP2/11|GP2/11]] chassis, which had been used by Formula 2 and its predecessor GP2 since [[2011 GP2 Series|2011]], was replaced by the [[Dallara F2 2018]].<ref name="new chassis">{{cite web|title=Further details of next-generation F2 car revealed|url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/formula-2-new-car-details-2018-932672/|last=Kalinauckas|first=Alex|work=[[motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=20 July 2017|access-date=20 July 2017}}</ref> It was powered by a fuel-efficient {{Convert|3.4|L|cuin|0|lk=on|abbr=off}} V6 single-turbocharged direct-injected engine developed by [[Mecachrome]] called V634T.<ref name="mecachrome">{{cite web|title=Exclusive: Mecachrome applies for Formula 1 engine tender|url=https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/exclusive-mecachrome-applies-for-formula-1-engine-tender/|last=Noble|first=Jonathan|work=[[motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=24 November 2015|access-date=24 November 2015}}</ref> The aging [[naturally-aspirated engine|naturally-aspirated]] [[V8 engine|V8]] engine and [[Dallara GP2/11]] chassis which were used since 2005 and 2011 respectively were permanently retired. Despite the generational change of chassis and engines, [[Elf Aquitaine|Elf]] would continue as a preferred official fuel partner and supplier of [[FIA Formula 2 Championship]] and also [[Pirelli]] FIA Formula 2 tyre sizes would remained same as pre-2017 [[Formula One tyres]]. In addition, the [[Pirelli]] FIA Formula 2 hard tyre colour would repainted from orange to ice blue in a reference of 2018 Formula One dry slick tyre compounds.

===Sporting regulations===
Teams were required to use the [[2018 FIA Formula One World Championship#Driver safety|"halo" cockpit protection device]], a [[Furcula|wishbone]]-shaped frame mounted above and around the driver's head and anchored to the monocoque forward of the cockpit.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://speedcafe.com/fia-take-cockpit-protection-categories/|title=FIA to take cockpit protection to other categories|date=24 August 2017|access-date=15 December 2017|website=[[Speedcafe|speedcafe.com]]|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170824093222/http://www.speedcafe.com/2017/08/24/fia-take-cockpit-protection-categories/|archive-date=24 August 2017|url-status=live}}</ref> The halo was designed to improve safety standards by deflecting debris away from a driver's head and was originally developed for use in [[Formula One]] before its application was expanded to other [[open-wheel]] championships and it was incorporated into the final design of the [[Dallara F2 2018]] chassis.<ref name="Entry"/><ref name="halo">{{cite news|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/131537/new-f2-car-revealed-to-feature-halo-device|title=New F2 car revealed, to feature halo device|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|date=31 August 2017|access-date=15 December 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170901211431/https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/131537/new-f2-car-revealed-to-feature-halo-device|archive-date=1 September 2017|url-status=dead}}</ref>

===Other changes===
The championship discontinued the use of [[promotional model]]s or "grid girls". The decision was made by Formula One management in the face of changing social attitudes and the decision by other sports to phase out the use of models,<ref>{{cite news|url=http://mobile.abc.net.au/news/2018-02-01/f1-axes-grid-girls-from-formula-one-races/9381902|title=Formula One axes grid girls from races to keep up with 'modern day societal norms'|first=Alle|last=McMahon|work=abc.net.au|publisher=[[Australian Broadcasting Corporation]]|date=1 February 2018|access-date=1 February 2018}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2018/01/31/motorsport/formula-one-to-stop-using-grid-girls-2018/index.html|title=Formula One to stop using grid girls in 2018|work=cnn.com|publisher=[[CNN]]|date=1 February 2018|access-date=1 February 2018}}</ref> and the decision applied to all categories appearing on the Grand Prix support bill, including Formula 2.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://speedcafe.com/formula-1-bans-grid-girls/|title=Formula 1 bans grid girls|work=[[Speedcafe|speedcafe.com]]|date=1 February 2018|access-date=3 February 2018|archive-date=3 February 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180203070438/https://www.speedcafe.com/2018/02/01/formula-1-bans-grid-girls/|url-status=live}}</ref>

===Team changes===
Twenty entries spread across ten teams were entered into the championship.<ref name="entry 2">{{cite news|last=Kalinauckas|first=Alex|date=8 February 2018|title=Formula 2 shuffles teams entered for 2018 season|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/134286/f2-shuffles-teams-entered-for-2018|access-date=9 February 2018}}</ref> [[Carlin Motorsport|Carlin]] returned to Formula 2 in 2018 after a one-year absence, while former [[World Series Formula V8 3.5]] team [[Charouz Racing System]] joined the championship. Charouz left the Formula 3.5 V8 championship when the championship was discontinued and the team subsequently submitted an entry to join the Formula 2 grid which marked the return of a Czech single-seater junior team to the second tier of [[Formula One]]'s feeder championships since the [[2003 International Formula 3000 season]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Hudson|first=Joe|date=17 November 2017|title=World Series Formula V8 3.5 to end after 2017 season|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|url=https://www.autosport.com/fv8+3.5/news/133074/formula-v8-35-to-end-after-2017-season|access-date=17 November 2017}}</ref> [[Racing Engineering]] were included on the initial entry list but later withdrew from the championship to focus on their [[European Le Mans Series]] entry.<ref name="entry 2" /> [[Fortec Motorsport]]s were due to make their Formula 2 début, replacing the departing [[Rapax Team|Rapax]] team, but later withdrew their entry due to a lack of sponsorship.<ref name="entry 2" /> Fortec were later granted permission to defer their entry by a year and were scheduled to make their début in 2019, but aborted the plans.<ref>{{cite news|last=Kalinauckas|first=Alex|date=24 February 2018|title=Fortec Formula 2 entry on hold for one year|work=[[Autosport|autosport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|url=https://www.autosport.com/f2/news/134519/fortec-f2-entry-on-hold-for-one-year|access-date=24 February 2018}}</ref>

====Mid-season changes====
[[Santino Ferrucci]] was banned for two rounds as punishment for multiple violations of the sporting and technical regulations—including deliberately nudging Arjun Maini on the cool down lap after the [[Silverstone Circuit|Silverstone]] round.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Benyon|first1=Jack|date=8 July 2018|title=Ferrucci handed four-race ban for hitting teammate|work=[[motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/ferrucci-handed-ban-for-deliberate-maini-hit-1057775/|access-date=8 July 2018}}</ref> He was later dismissed by [[Trident Racing|Trident]], with the team citing sponsorship issues rather than his suspension as the reason for Ferrucci's firing.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Benyon|first1=Jack|date=18 July 2018|title=Trident terminates banned Ferrucci's contract|work=[[Motorsport.com]]|publisher=[[Motorsport Network]]|url=https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/trident-dumps-banned-ferrucci-1061134/|access-date=18 July 2018|archive-date=18 July 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180718205037/https://www.motorsport.com/fia-f2/news/trident-dumps-banned-ferrucci-1061134/|url-status=dead}}</ref> Ferrucci was replaced by Trident's [[2018 GP3 Series|GP3 Series]] driver [[Alessio Lorandi]] for the [[2018 Hungaroring FIA Formula 2 round|Hungaroring round]].<ref name="Lorandi" />

==Championship standings==
===Scoring system===
Points were awarded to the top 10 classified finishers in the Feature race, and to the top 8 classified finishers in the Sprint race. The pole-sitter in the feature race also received four points, and two points were given to the driver who set the fastest lap inside the top ten in both the feature and sprint races. No extra points are awarded to the pole-sitter in the sprint race as the grid for the sprint race is based on the results of the feature race with the top eight drivers having their positions reversed.<ref name=Regulations>{{cite web|title=2018 FIA Formula 2 Sporting Regulations|url=https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2018_fia_f2_sporting_regulations_v5_07-06-18.pdf|work=[[Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile]]|pages=3-4|date=7 June 2018|access-date=17 March 2025|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20221011223201/https://www.fia.com/sites/default/files/2018_fia_f2_sporting_regulations_v5_07-06-18.pdf|archive-date=11 October 2022|url-status=dead}}</ref>
