The '''2020 Euroformula Open Championship''' was a multi-event [[Motorsport#Motor racing|motor racing]] championship for single-seater [[Open-wheel car|open wheel]] [[Formula racing|formula racing cars]] that was held across [[Europe]]. The championship featured drivers competing in the new [[Dallara]] 320 car, which features the [[Halo (safety device)|Halo safety device]].<ref name="Calendar"/> It was the seventh [[Euroformula Open Championship]] season.

CryptoTower Racing Team driver [[Yifei Ye]] won the title after the second race at Barcelona, having won 11 races, 13 pole positions, 12 fastest lap.

==Race calendar==
A nine-round provisional calendar was revealed on 11 October 2019.  While [[Silverstone Circuit]] was not on this season's calendar, [[Autodromo di Pergusa]] was scheduled to debut as a Euroformula Open Championship round.<ref name="Calendar">{{cite news |last1=Wood |first1=Elliot |title=Mediterranean Grand Prix revived by Euroformula Open for 2020 |url=http://www.formulascout.com/mediterranean-grand-prix-revived-by-euroformula-open-for-2020/55379 |accessdate=11 October 2019 |work=formulascout.com |publisher=Formula Scout |date=11 October 2019}}</ref>  An updated calendar was announced on 22 October 2019.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3794/2020-euroformula-calendar-announced-gp-del-mediterraneo-joins-pau-gp-and-seven-top-f1-tracks|title=2020 Euroformula calendar announced, GP del Mediterraneo joins Pau GP and seven top F1 tracks|work=euroformulaopen.net|date=22 October 2019|accessdate=24 October 2019}}</ref>

After the start of the season was delayed due to the [[COVID-19 pandemic|2019-20 coronavirus pandemic]], a new 8-round calendar was announced on 21 May 2020.  The round at [[Pau Grand Prix|Circuit de Pau-Ville]], which was on the original schedule, was not run this season.<ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3920/euroformula-confirms-8-rounds-and-includes-mugello-and-jarama-in-2020-calendar|title=Euroformula confirms 8 rounds and includes Mugello and Jarama in 2020 calendar|work=euroformulaopen.net|accessdate=21 May 2020}}</ref>  On 3 June 2020 it was announced that the season opener at [[Hungaroring]] was moved back to 23–26 July.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3923/euroformula-open-season-opener-at-the-hungaroring-moved-to-23-26-july|title=Euroformula Open season-opener at the Hungaroring moved to 23-26 July|date=3 June 2020}}</ref>  On 10 June 2020 the season opener was moved to 8–9 August, while the round at [[Autodromo di Pergusa]] was cancelled with plans to run there in 2021.<ref>{{Cite news|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/3929/the-2020-euroformula-open-season-opener-at-the-hungaroring-to-take-place-on-6-9-august|title=The 2020 Euroformula Open season-opener at the Hungaroring to take place on 6-9 August|date=10 June 2020}}</ref> Two of the eight rescheduled events will hold three races to compensate for the loss of one round.<ref>{{Cite web|title=Monza welcomes the Dallara 320 for the first time with three races|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/4037/monza-welcomes-the-dallara-320-for-the-first-time-with-three-races|access-date=2020-09-23|website=www.euroformulaopen.net|language=en}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://www.euroformulaopen.net/en/news/4091/magnificent-spa-hosts-euroformula-s-second-triple-header|title=Magnificent Spa hosts Euroformula's second triple-header|website=www.euroformulaopen.net|date=14 October 2020|accessdate=15 October 2020}}</ref> On 22 October, the planned season finale at [[Circuito del Jarama|Jarama]] was cancelled due to rising COVID-19 cases in Europe, and it was announced that Barcelona would hold four races instead.<ref>{{Cite web|last=Wood|first=Elliot|date=2020-10-22|title=Euroformula cancels Jarama season finale, Barcelona to host four races|url=https://formulascout.com/euroformula-cancels-jarama-season-finale-barcelona-to-host-four-races/69841|access-date=2020-10-22|website=Formula
